Career Vision & Strategy

Where I'm headed, and why

An honest account of the gap between where I am and where senior finance leadership requires me to be.

Where I'm headed

My long-term goal is senior finance leadership, and eventually a CFO-level role. I'm not there yet, and I'm not presenting myself as though I am — but I've spent six years building the part of that role that's hardest to fake: technical credibility. IFRS reporting, Saudi tax and regulatory compliance, internal controls, and audit-readiness across multi-entity operations are now a genuine strength, evidenced by zero material findings across every external audit I've supported and a top-in-country result in ACCA's Advanced Audit & Assurance paper.

What I don't yet have — and I'd rather say this plainly than let a CV imply otherwise — is the strategic finance, corporate planning, and cross-border commercial exposure that senior finance leadership requires. That's the deliberate reason I've submitted my application to the University of Edinburgh Online MBA.

Why this MBA, specifically

The Edinburgh Online MBA is built for exactly the gap I've identified in my own development: it strengthens strategic finance and corporate planning, commercial decision-making, international business exposure, and leadership capability — all while letting me continue building real-world evidence in my current role rather than pausing my career to study. I'm not looking for a credential to add to a list. I'm looking for the specific capabilities that turn six years of technical depth into the judgment a CFO role actually requires.

The next 5–10 years

Near term: complete the CIA certification, build FP&A and business-partnering experience directly, and progress the Edinburgh MBA application and studies.
Medium term: move into a role where financial analysis directly informs commercial and operational decisions — FP&A, finance business partnering, or a senior finance role with genuine strategic input.
Longer term: senior finance leadership, with the eventual goal of a CFO-level role — and, in parallel, building the commercial and financial discipline to eventually support or build businesses of my own, informed by exactly the controls-and-compliance rigor most founders learn the hard way.
