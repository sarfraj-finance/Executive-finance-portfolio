Professional Story

Why this path, specifically

I got into accounting for an unglamorous reason: I liked that it had a right answer. A trial balance either balances or it doesn't, and I found that kind of clarity satisfying in a way that stuck with me through my commerce education in India. What I didn't expect was how much judgment actually sits behind that apparent certainty once you're doing the work for real.

I moved to Saudi Arabia because it offered something most early-career finance roles don't: real responsibility, fast. Multi-entity, multi-currency operations in a market with genuinely demanding regulatory expectations meant I was reconciling real consequences from early on, not shadowing someone else's sign-off. That environment is where my technical foundation actually got built — not in a classroom, but in filing cycles that either held up or didn't.

ACCA was the natural next step once I realized how much I didn't yet know I didn't know. I wanted a qualification that would test judgment, not just recall — and Advanced Audit & Assurance in particular forced me to understand controls and assurance from first principles rather than as a checklist. Passing it with the highest mark in Saudi Arabia confirmed the approach was working; it didn't change the approach itself.

Strategic finance and FP&A are where I'm heading now, for a straightforward reason: I've spent six years making sure numbers are accurate, compliant, and defensible. I want to spend the next phase making sure they're also useful — shaping a decision before it's made, not just documenting it afterward. That's not a rejection of the technical work. It's the next thing built on top of it.

Finance Philosophy

How I think, not just what I know

Finance creates better decisions

Numbers are only valuable once they change what happens next.

Compliance creates trust

Controls done well protect the speed a business already has, rather than slowing it down.

Cash flow creates resilience

A profitable business can still run out of cash. I watch both, not just the one that looks better on paper.

Data tells the business story

A number without context is just a number — the story is what makes it decision-useful.

Controls enable growth

Growth without controls is just exposure that hasn't surfaced yet.

Automation creates analysis time

Less time spent reconciling manually means more time spent interpreting what the numbers mean.

Commercial understanding matters

Finance disconnected from how the business actually operates is just bookkeeping with extra steps.
